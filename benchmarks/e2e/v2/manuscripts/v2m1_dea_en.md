# Low-voltage dielectric elastomer actuators for wearable counterpressure and haptic devices

## Introduction

Dielectric elastomer actuators (DEAs) are used in soft robotics, medical devices, and wearable electronics (Mei et al., 2026). A DEA-based mechanical counterpressure cuff for space suits reached a counterpressure of 19.52 MPa with a response time of 0.7 s (Veloso et al., 2025). Fillers raise the permittivity of silicone matrices: onion-like carbons gave a maximum actuated strain of 25.9% at 32 kV/mm (Zeng & Tang, 2026), and MXene/onion-like-carbon hybrids kept the composites stable up to about 400 °C (Tang & Zeng, 2026). Dielectric liquid crystal elastomer actuators deliver enhanced work output only under small loads below 1.5 MPa (Annapooranan et al., 2026).

At the device level, a rolled DEA drove a soft crawling robot at 4 mm/s at 7 kV and 2 Hz (Wang et al., 2026), in-plane DEAs enabled resonant locomotion of thin hybrid robots (Wang et al., 2025), and bistable actuators were built by combining DEAs with shape memory polymer composites (Niu et al., 2023). Arrays of DEAs provide haptic feedback in wearables (Croce et al., 2025), conical DEA generators harvest wave energy (Gurjar & Patra, 2026), and DEA lenses give tunable focal length (Zhang et al., 2026). Electrode-free ionic skin layers have also been proposed for haptic sleeves (Hartmann et al., 2025), and robotic systems now automate DEA manufacturing (Remenar et al., 2026).
