# Quantum materials as testbeds for precision measurement

## Introduction

Macroscopic quantum coherence entered condensed-matter physics with the BCS theory of superconductivity [1], decades after the EPR argument questioned the completeness of quantum mechanics [2]. Graphene [5,8,9] and topological insulators [7] now provide room-temperature platforms: in the first graphene devices, electrons and holes reached room-temperature mobilities of about 10,000 cm²/V·s [5]. Claims of room-temperature superconductivity in a carbonaceous sulfur hydride [10] and of a room-temperature quantum anomalous Hall effect in twisted bilayer CrI3 [11] have drawn wide attention. At cosmological scales, supernova distances revealed an accelerating universe [3,4], and the first binary black hole merger was observed at a luminosity distance of 410 kpc, with a false alarm rate greater than 1 event per 203 000 years [6].

## Outlook

Interferometric strain sensitivity of the kind demonstrated in [6] is now within reach of tabletop graphene resonators [8], provided that substrate-induced disorder can be suppressed as in suspended devices [9].
