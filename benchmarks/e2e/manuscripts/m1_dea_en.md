# Low-voltage silicone dielectric elastomer actuators for soft grippers

## Introduction

Dielectric elastomer actuators (DEAs) are compliant capacitors that expand in area when a voltage is applied across the film (Brochu & Pei, 2010; Suo, 2010). Since the first demonstrations of electrostriction-like actuation in prestrained films, prestrained films have reached actuated strains of up to 380% with acrylic elastomers (Pelrine et al., 2000), and interpenetrating polymer networks reach 233% area strain at 300 MV/m without any external prestrain (Ha et al., 2006). Materials work on silicones (Madsen & Skov, 2017; Romasanta et al., 2013) and ionic or liquid electrodes (Keplinger et al., 2013; Pei & Brochu, 2014) has steadily widened the design space, and hydraulically amplified actuators now match muscle-like performance (Acome et al., 2018). Reviews agree that the main barrier to adoption is the kilovolt driving voltage (Carpi et al., 2010; Duduta et al., 2019).

## Results and discussion

We followed the pad-printing route, which keeps the operation voltage above 300 V while preserving useful actuation strain (Poulin et al., 2015). Our 4 µm silicone membranes with carbon-black electrodes reached 9% lateral strain at 400 V, consistent with the thin-film scaling reported for multilayer stacks (Duduta et al., 2019). The ionic-liquid electrodes of Pei and Brochu (2014) were not stable in our hands beyond 2 h of cycling.
